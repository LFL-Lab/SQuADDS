squadds.components.export_fixups module#

Post-processing that reproduces the tl-squadds star dataset GDS export (exp11 / exp12).

The star layouts of the tl-squadds dataset were written by two versions of the squadds2-helpers export, the second in two environments. style selects one:

"original" (exp10, and exp11 as first written 2026-09-03..09)

Metal GDS renderer output (gdstk; polygons over 199 points fractured on write), pieces OR-ed back per body.

"reexport" (173 exp11 designs rewritten 2026-09-24..28; quantum-metal gdstk renderer, shapely 2.1 / GEOS 3.13) and "reexport_gdspy" (all of exp12, written 2026-09-29; gdspy-based qiskit-metal 0.1.x renderer, shapely 2.0 / GEOS 3.11)

Reconstructed from the dataset files (the export script is not in squadds2-helpers); each step is checked vertex by vertex against the dataset GDS:

  1. polygons are fractured at 199 points: by the gdstk renderer (reexport), or as the gdspy renderer does it (reexport_gdspy): gdspy.Polygon(exterior incl. closing point).fracture(199, 1e-9) for the bodies and gdspy.boolean(chip, cap_etch, "not", precision=1e-9, max_points=199) for the ground, snapped to 1 nm and the ground pieces OR-ed back. gdspy and gdstk place the cuts (and 1 nm boolean vertices) differently.

  2. pieces are re-assembled with the helpers’ _merge_cap_polys_by_shared_points: grouped by shared vertices or overlap, then OR-ed one at a time into every partial result. When the first two pieces of a group only touch, their OR returns both and the next piece is OR-ed into each, so the group yields two overlapping polygons. Of all polygons the export kept the largest n_bodies (star + enabled pads): this drops the smaller duplicate, i.e. the part of a pad beyond a fracture line (a straight chord, up to ~3,300 um^2), and any sliver piece that touched nothing.

  3. each body is reoriented clockwise (first vertex kept) and simplified by Douglas-Peucker at 10 nm (shapely, preserve_topology=False; if that splits a sliver off, the largest part is kept), which removes ~70 % of the pad vertices (the erode/dilate near-duplicates).

  4. every body is isolated from the ground: ground minus body.buffer(50 nm, join_style="bevel"). An unfed pad’s lead ends on the ground cut-out, so this leaves a 50 nm slit (35 nm across the bevelled corners) at its end; the fed pad (port etch) and the star are not affected.

The vertices also depend on the GEOS version that built the component (1 nm vertex choices, hence fracture cuts): exp12 reproduces exactly only under shapely 2.0.x (GEOS 3.11), exp11 only under shapely 2.1 (GEOS 3.13).

The component geometry itself (GeneralizedCapStar) is unchanged. Polygons are gdstk polygons in nm.

Bases: QComponent

Two-terminal concentric capacitor with elliptical inner and outer bodies.

Traces (from inside out):

  1. Inner body outer edge (filled pad)

  2. Outer-body inner edge (ring hole)

  3. Outer-body outer edge (ring outline)

  4. Ground-plane inner edge (third ellipse) — gap between outer body and ground

When inner_feed is True (default), a south CPW reaches the inner pad through a clearance slot in the ring. When False, the outer ring stays continuous. A north CPW attaches to the outer ring. min_trace_gap is enforced between consecutive traces and around the inner feed.

Inspired by the circular pads in Qiskit Metal TransmonConcentric, rewritten as a lumped capacitor with independent elliptical traces.

squadds.components.generalized_cap_star module#

Generalized multi-terminal star capacitor (Wallraff / Surface-17 style).

Central n-pointed star plus up to n peripheral connector pads. Disabled pad sectors are filled by the ground plane while keeping an equidistant gap around the star. Each enabled pad gets a radial CPW stub / pin.

The tl-squadds star datasets (exp11, exp12) were written by an export step that post-processes this geometry; squadds.components.export_fixups reproduces it.

class squadds.components.generalized_cap_star.GeneralizedCapStar(*args, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: QComponent

Star capacitor with optional peripheral connector pads.

Bodies:

  • star — central n-pointed star (always present)

  • pad_i — peripheral pads for each enabled connector (i = 0 .. n-1)

Pads sit in the valleys between fingers: each pad is disk(star_outer) minus star.buffer(gap_i) in that valley, so it matches the gap angle, keeps an equidistant clearance to the star, and stops at the finger tips. The ground opening is a circle of pocket_radius. Empty valleys are either left open to that circle (valley_ground=circle) or filled by ground with an equidistant gap to the star (valley_ground=fill), so tip outer arcs follow the star tip fillets at radius tip_radius + gap via star.buffer(gap). CPW stubs add a side gap only outside the capacitor pocket (cpw_width / cpw_gap), with a flat open end. Per-pad star gaps via pad_gaps; omitted => gap.

Any

squadds.components.shielded_cap_concentric module#

class squadds.components.shielded_cap_concentric.ShieldedCapConcentric(*args, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: QComponent

Concentric capacitor with guard rings between the inner disk and the outer ring.

From the centre out: an inner disk (one conductor), one or more guard rings, an outer ring fed by a north CPW (another conductor) and the circular ground opening. Each guard ring is either floating (its own conductor) or grounded, in which case a radial strap joins it to the ground plane through slots in every ring outside it, so the outer ring becomes a split ring. All outlines are circles stretched by aspect along x.

Options (lengths with units; lists comma-separated, one entry per guard ring from the inside out):

  • inner_radius: radius of the inner disk

  • aspect: x/y stretch of every outline

  • guard_gaps, guard_widths: gap to the previous body and width of each guard ring

class squadds.components.xmon.Xmon(*args, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: TransmonCross

Xmon capacitor: a four-arm cross in a ground pocket with one or two claw couplers.

The geometry is quantum-metal’s TransmonCross (cross, cross etch and claw connectors, pins included) without the junction, so the layout is the capacitive network of an Xmon: the cross is one conductor and every claw another. The readout claw sits on the north arm by default; add a second claw with another entry in connection_pads (for example bus with connector_location "0" (west) or "180" (east); as in the dataset, any angle above 135 is the east arm).

Pins are quantum-metal’s: the claw pin line runs from -claw_cpw_width/2 to +claw_width/2 across the claw feed, so its middle is offset from the feed axis when the two widths differ. The dataset’s port stubs and ground end slots were cut at these pins.

This is the component of the tl-squadds Xmon family (exp13). It differs from squadds.components.qubits.TransmonCross, which draws the SQuADDS qubit with its junction options.

class squadds.components.GeneralizedCapStar(*args, **kwargs)[source]#

Bases: QComponent

Star capacitor with optional peripheral connector pads.

Bodies:

  • star — central n-pointed star (always present)

  • pad_i — peripheral pads for each enabled connector (i = 0 .. n-1)

Pads sit in the valleys between fingers: each pad is disk(star_outer) minus star.buffer(gap_i) in that valley, so it matches the gap angle, keeps an equidistant clearance to the star, and stops at the finger tips. The ground opening is a circle of pocket_radius. Empty valleys are either left open to that circle (valley_ground=circle) or filled by ground with an equidistant gap to the star (valley_ground=fill), so tip outer arcs follow the star tip fillets at radius tip_radius + gap via star.buffer(gap). CPW stubs add a side gap only outside the capacitor pocket (cpw_width / cpw_gap), with a flat open end. Per-pad star gaps via pad_gaps; omitted => gap.
